FIRST-PERSON VOICE CORRECTION

Write this article in my voice, as though I am speaking directly to a prospective client visiting my personal portfolio.

I am Edward Mavungah, a Botswana-based developer offering website development, custom business software, AI automation, cloud solutions, and data engineering.

Replace awkward third-person references to me with natural first-person language throughout the article, headings, project descriptions, author introduction, and calls to action.

VOICE RULES

1. Use “I,” “me,” and “my” when describing my work, experience, services, approach, availability, and projects.

2. Address the reader as “you” and refer to “your business,” “your website,” “your team,” and “your project.”

3. Do not refer to me as “Edward,” “Edward Mavungah,” “he,” “him,” “the developer,” or “the author” in the article’s narrative.

4. Do not use “we,” “our team,” or “our agency” unless I have explicitly supplied information about a team. Present me honestly as an individual practitioner.

5. My name may appear where identification is appropriate, such as an author byline or a brief introduction:
   “I’m Edward Mavungah, a Botswana-based developer building websites, business software, and AI-powered applications.”
   Do not repeat that introduction throughout the article.

6. Keep general educational explanations neutral. Do not force “I” into every paragraph or turn every explanation into a sales pitch.

7. Preserve the article’s useful information, search intent, country-specific context, natural keywords, citations, and logical structure. Rewrite headings naturally rather than preserving an awkward exact-match keyword.

8. Preserve the distinction between:
   - What I have actually built.
   - What I offer as a service.
   - What is a hypothetical example.
   - What is general industry guidance.

9. Changing the voice must not create stronger claims. Do not turn a general statement about developers into a claim about my personal experience. Do not invent customers, results, qualifications, local offices, prices, or delivery promises.

10. Keep the tone confident, practical, approachable, and business-focused—not self-congratulatory or overly promotional.

REPLACEMENT EXAMPLES

Instead of:
“Edward helps businesses build websites and custom software.”

Write:
“I help businesses build websites and custom software.”

Instead of:
“Edward’s approach starts with understanding your business.”

Write:
“I start by understanding your business and the problem you need to solve.”

Instead of:
“Relevant Work by Edward Mavungah”

Write:
“Examples of My Work”

Instead of:
“Why Work With Edward?”

Write:
“How I Can Help Your Business”

Instead of:
“Can a Botswana-based developer work with my business in COUNTRY?”

Write:
“Can you work with my business in COUNTRY if you’re based in Botswana?”

Answer in my voice:
“Yes. I’m based in Botswana and available for remote website and software projects in COUNTRY.”

Instead of:
“Share your requirements with Edward.”

Write:
“Tell me what you need your website or software to do.”

Instead of:
“Explore Edward’s projects.”

Write:
“Explore my projects to see examples of what I build.”

Instead of:
“Tell Edward what your business does and what you want to improve.”

Write:
“Tell me what your business does, what you want to improve, and what you need your website or software to achieve.”

CTA STYLE

Write soft CTAs as natural invitations from me to the reader, for example:

- “If you’re unsure which option fits your business, I can help you clarify the scope.”
- “You can explore my projects to see how I approach similar problems.”
- “Share the workflow you want to improve, and we can discuss whether you need custom software or a simpler solution.”

Use “we” only for a genuinely shared activity between the reader and me, such as “we can discuss”—not to imply I have an agency or team.

End with one direct CTA in my voice:

“Tell me about your business, the problem you want to solve, and your target launch date. If you have a budget range, include that too so I can better understand the scope.”
